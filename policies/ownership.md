# Ownership

**Status: Draft**

Poimandres is a collective. Nobody owns it, including the people who started it, the people running it now, and the people writing this page. This page explains who owns what.

## What "owning" a project actually means

"I own this project" can mean five different things. They have different answers.

| Thing | Who has it | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Credit** for creating something | The person who created it, permanently | Kept in git history, READMEs, docs and our contributor/founder lists. We never erase or hide it. |
| **Copyright** in code you wrote | You, for the lines you wrote | You licensed those lines to everyone under the repo's open-source license (MIT for most of our repos). That license can't be taken back. If your code has since been rewritten or removed, you hold no rights over the code that replaced it. |
| **The repository** under `github.com/pmndrs` | Depends on whether the org has **adopted** it | **Hosted** repos (experiments and things people brought in) and **Stewarded** repos (held for safekeeping at someone's request) are still the maintainer's or owner's, and they can move them out any time. **Adopted** repos (once the org has taken responsibility, Tier 2 and up) belong to the collective and can't be transferred out. See [Repository tiers](./repository-tiers.md). |
| **The package name** on npm | Depends | `@pmndrs/...` scoped names always stay with the org. An unscoped name from a Hosted repo can go with it if the repo leaves (see [Repository tiers](./repository-tiers.md#moving-a-repo-out)). |
| **Admin / Owner access** (GitHub, npm, domains, 1Password, fiscal host) | Whoever currently holds the role that needs it | A role, not property, and not a title of honor or a reward for past work. It ends when the role ends, including after 6 months of inactivity. Who holds what: [Security → Who holds access](./security.md#who-holds-access). How roles end and how to come back: [Membership → Stepping away and coming back](./membership.md#stepping-away-and-coming-back). |

The short version: **you keep credit and your own copyright. Until the org adopts a repo, it's yours to take back. Once adopted, the collective keeps the repo, the name, the package, and the keys.** You're always free to fork any repo, at any tier, and build on it elsewhere under its own name.

## Project direction

A project's direction is set by the people currently maintaining it, under the rules for its tier (see [Repository tiers](./repository-tiers.md)). Past authorship doesn't give a veto.

If you want to take a project in a different direction, a fork is a normal, healthy outcome under the license. It lives outside the pmndrs org and should go out under its own name, so users aren't confused about which one is which.

Disagree with a decision? See [If you disagree](./membership.md#if-you-disagree) on the Membership page.
