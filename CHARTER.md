# Poimandres Charter

**Status: Draft**

## 1. Mission

Poimandres is an open source developer collective for creative technology and developer tooling. We make things for people who make things because we believe code can be a craft, a creative medium, and a source of joy. Our projects are sustained through shared effort, mutual support, and trust.

## 2. Core Principles

**Simplicity.** Software should be simple to understand and use, with clear models and elegant interfaces. The API and philosophy should fit in a single readme and be explained in three steps or less.

**Pragmatism.** Projects should solve demonstrated problems for a meaningful community of developers, avoiding speculative projects.

**Stability.** Software should earn long-term trust. Libraries refine over time but aim to be stable, mainly by being both simple and practical.

**Quality.** Projects should meet a high standard of craft. This includes implementation, documentation, design, and the overall developer experience.

## 3. How We Work

**Stewardship.** Members look after projects and resources on behalf of the collective, not as owners. Roles are temporary and can be handed off.

**Autonomy.** Projects run themselves. Leads own their roadmaps and releases, and the collective only steps in for what a project can’t or shouldn’t handle alone.

**Shared control.** No single point of failure for money, credentials, infrastructure, or decisions. Anything critical has more than one person who can act on it.

**Openness.** Decisions are made in the open and written down where people can find them. Sensitive access is limited to those who need it and is tracked.

**Light process.** Rules have to earn their place. We add process only when it solves a real problem, and lazy consensus is the default.

## 4. Membership

Membership is a working role within the collective. It is granted to contributors who need trusted access or take responsibility for the collective. Members are expected to uphold the collective’s mission and principles, use their access responsibly, and participate in decision making. Access is granted for a role and returned when the role ends.

<summary><strong>What are working roles?</strong></summary>

There is no strict definition of a working role, but we have a few basic outlines. The needs of the collective evolve over time, and so does what it means to be a member.

## 5. Decision-Making

Decisions should be made as close as possible to the work they affect. Project leads and maintainers decide for their own projects, including project status.

The Steering Committee resolves project deadlocks, financial, security, or multi-project issues. Any steering decision can be [appealed to the collective](./policies/membership.md#if-you-disagree).

Collective decisions use **lazy consensus**. A proposal must be presented in a designated collective communication channel and is accepted if no member objects within five days. An objection pauses the proposal until it is resolved.

<details>
<summary><strong>What are collective decisions?</strong></summary>

Anything that reasonably affects the collective as a whole is a collective decision. This includes adopting projects, adopting initiatives, and allocating collective resources.

</details>

If a project no longer has an active lead, the collective may make decisions for it.

## 6. Adopting Projects

Projects the collective adopts must follow the collective’s mission and principles. A project that meets some of them but not all can still be adopted if members agree.

<details>
<summary><strong>Example criteria</strong></summary>

An adopted project should have a clearly defined purpose and fit into one of the collective’s initiatives. These example questions can help test whether a project fits the collective:

- **Simplicity:** Is the project’s purpose simple and clear enough to explain in a couple of sentences?
- **Pragmatism:** Does the project solve a real problem shared by developers beyond a single use case, product, or sponsor?
- **Stability:** Is the project’s API simple and settled enough to support long-term use?
- **Quality:** Does the project stand out in some way (API, depth, design, etc.)?

</details>

## 7. Initiatives

The collective may adopt initiatives to set priorities, allocate collective resources, and align work toward the collective’s mission. An initiative has a definite goal and should not be meant to last indefinitely. An initiative must have a lead dev to be accepted.

<details>
<summary><strong>Example initiatives</strong></summary>

A good initiative should define its **motivations**, **goals**, **scope of work**, and the **resources** it requires. It should work as a document that can be shared to explain the work we are doing and how someone can contribute.

Initiatives can cover major releases for projects, infrastructure changes within the collective, or support for new use cases or tooling.

</details>

## 8. Project Policy

Projects must have their status clearly marked. The status tells people how committed the collective is to the project and what updates to expect. See [Repository tiers](./policies/repository-tiers.md).

## 9. Money

Funds are held by our fiscal host, currently Open Source Collective. Every expense is requested and recorded publicly there.

- At least two Steering members are admins on the account.
- Funds less than $300 for operations and occasional expenses are expected and at the Steering Committee's discretion. Beyond that, collective approval is required.
